## Shared measures and stop rule

The primary outcome is adverse actions: distinct errors that could cause a wrong or unauthorized business decision, disclosure, identity merge, loss of a required check, or suppression of a material conflict. Agree on examples and severity with an independent scorer before the run. Cite each event to source and output, count it once, and retain blocked or prevented actions separately from completed actions.

Secondary outcomes are useful result, status navigation, total human effort/time, and review time. Define useful result against the task's predeclared acceptance before assignment. Status navigation asks whether an operator who did not do the capture can find current supported status, source, open issues and next action from retained material. Record total task time and active effort separately; review time includes independent source checking and correction. Include participant, operator, facilitator, reviewer, repair and coordination effort. Do not omit preparation or rework.

Stop further candidate exposure if candidate adverse actions exceed the matched baseline. Preserve the failing evidence and route it to the responsible reviewer. Do not offset the excess with time saved, answer completeness or participant preference. Equal counts do not prove non-inferiority. Any unsupported owner, approval or rule authority is a hard failure and receives no favorable average.

Every comparative gate needs a same-scope manual-baseline record or case, prepared independently with the declared current procedure. Do not show the same case or message to one operator in both arms. Score candidate and baseline together under masked labels. If a defensible pair cannot be prepared, leave comparative outcomes and the stop-rule comparison `open`.

## Gate A — unaccompanied first use and repair

Recruit 3–5 people who have not previously used the candidate procedure. Run each separately on a fresh, bounded case with the same frozen first-use instructions in the candidate arm. Prepare a same-scope manual-baseline case through an independent operator; no operator sees the same case in both arms. No facilitator gives hints. A silent observer may record time and steps; intervene only to prevent an unsafe action, then record the intervention and mark that task's unassisted outcome separately.

Score task completion, source traceability, preservation of `open` gaps, ability to find status and next action, adverse actions, total effort/time and review time. If a user gets stuck, retain the exact pre-repair state and blocker. Identify the single responsible home for the defect, make the smallest documented correction, and test it with a fresh user or clean session. Do not coach the original user and count the coached result as first-use evidence. Keep pre- and post-repair results separate and bind each to its actual revision.

This small first-use screen can expose repair needs; it cannot estimate the broader user population. If any stop rule fires, stop candidate exposure. Otherwise report every participant case separately; do not call the gate passed from an average alone.

## Gate B — second-operator continuation after seven elapsed days

At least seven full elapsed days (168 hours) after a first operator freezes a case record, give the retained router and authorized candidate record to a different operator who did not participate in capture. Give no verbal briefing, chat history or private memory. Ask that operator to answer the frozen reuse questions, identify supported status and gaps, and name the next allowed action from those retained sources alone. Run the same continuation protocol on a same-scope manual-baseline case with a different second operator; neither operator sees the other's case.

Score source and revision traceability, correctness of current status, recovery of `open` questions, next-action scope, adverse actions, total effort/time and independent review time. Record actual capture and handoff timestamps to establish elapsed time. If a needed link, source or revision is absent, score the gap and leave the dependent use `open`; do not fill it from the original operator. Any repair requires a new revision and a later fresh continuation case.

## Gate C — narrow domain review

Before review, declare one reviewer's demonstrated domain and decision scope, the claims within that scope and the claims explicitly outside it. Provide a blind copy of each candidate and manual-baseline result with its cited evidence. Ask the reviewer only to assess meaning, missing conditions, contradictions and fit for that bounded use. Do not treat review as proof that the person owns a source, rule or approval decision. If actual authority matters, obtain evidence from its responsible source separately.

Score supported findings, missed material conditions, unsupported claims, unresolved disagreements, adverse actions, review time and total effort. Keep out-of-scope conclusions `open` and report them as routing inputs. Preserve reviewer comments and corrections as separate evidence; do not silently change the scored answer.

## Gate D — matched business comparison

Choose a bounded recurring task and a manual baseline. Before assignment, define the business result, recipient, case-selection frame, period, unit, source revisions, necessary checks, baseline procedure, candidate procedure and stop rule. Pair cases that meet the same declared scope and compare equivalent tasks. Counterbalance case-to-arm assignment and order; do not give one operator the same case in both arms. Keep task instructions and available inputs equivalent. The scorer remains blind to arm.

Score the primary adverse-action outcome first. Then score useful result and status navigation against the predeclared acceptance. Measure total human effort/time and review time for each arm, including setup, clarification, correction and coordination. Report paired cases and missing inputs separately; do not merge populations or time periods that do not match. If no defensible matched pair, baseline or outcome definition exists, do not run or claim a comparison; record the missing decision/evidence and leave the gate `open`.
